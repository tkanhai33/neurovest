# Diversification and concentration risk

Diversification means spreading exposure across different companies,
industries, regions, and sometimes asset types. It can reduce
company-specific risk: a product failure, accounting scandal, labour
dispute, or management mistake at one company has less influence on
the whole portfolio when the investor owns other holdings.

Diversification cannot eliminate market-wide risk. Recessions, major
interest-rate changes, inflation shocks, and geopolitical events can
affect many assets at the same time. During stressed markets,
correlations can rise, so holdings that normally move differently may
still fall together.

Concentration risk is the risk of depending too heavily on one
company, sector, country, theme, or source of income. A concentrated
position can create larger gains and larger losses. Investors should
understand what is driving the concentration, how much loss they could
tolerate, and whether other holdings genuinely offset the same risk.

This is educational research context, not a recommendation to buy,
sell, or hold any security.
