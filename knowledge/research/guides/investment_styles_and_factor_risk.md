# Value, growth, momentum, and factor risk

Value investing generally looks for companies priced low relative to
measures such as earnings, cash flow, assets, or book value. Growth
investing generally emphasizes companies expected to grow revenue or
earnings faster than average. Neither style is automatically better:
each can lag for long periods and can behave differently as rates,
economic growth, and investor expectations change.

Factor investing groups common return patterns into themes such as
market exposure, size, value, profitability, investment, quality, and
momentum. A factor may have a long historical record without offering
a smooth or guaranteed return in the future.

Momentum investing follows relative strength or persistent trends.
Its practical risks include reversals, high turnover, trading costs,
crowded positions, and long stretches of underperformance. A useful
review asks whether the rule still works after realistic costs,
different market periods, and a reasonable implementation delay.

This is educational research context, not individualized financial
advice.
